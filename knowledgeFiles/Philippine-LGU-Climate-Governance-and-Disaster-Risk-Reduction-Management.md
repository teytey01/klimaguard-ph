

Philippine LGU Climate Governance and Disaster Risk Reduction
Management Frameworks: Templates, Forms, and Implementation
## Guidelines
## 1 Executive Summary
The Philippines has established a comprehensive, legally mandated framework for LGU-level climate governance
and disaster risk reduction management under RA 9729 and RA 10121, operationalized through standardized
templates and protocols applicable across all LGU tiers—from provinces to barangays. The system comprises
five interlocking components: the CCC-HLURB 6-step CDRA methodology for hazard and vulnerability assess-
ment, which feeds into both Comprehensive Land Use Plans and the five-section LCCAP template; the LDRRMC/
BDRRMC institutional structure organized around four thematic pillars (prevention, preparedness, response, re-
covery); the DANA reporting system with its mandatory 3-hour initial reporting deadline and 14 standardized Es-
sential Elements of Information; the 70/30 LDRRMF allocation rule dedicating 5% of regular revenues to disaster
management; and the COA audit framework governed by FDP Form 8 and Circulars 2014-002 and 2014-009.

2 Introduction and Legal Framework
The Philippines, one of the world’s most disaster-prone countries, has established a comprehensive legal and
institutional framework for climate governance and disaster risk reduction management (DRRM) at the local gov-
ernment unit (LGU) level. Two landmark laws anchor this system:Republic Act No. 9729(Climate Change Act of
2009), which designates LGUs as“frontline agencies in the formulation, planning and implementation of climate
changeactionplans”[8],andRepublicActNo.10121(PhilippineDRRMActof2010),whichmandatesthecreation
of Local DRRM Councils at provincial, city, and municipal levels, and Barangay DRRM Committees at the barangay
level [
## 9].
These laws are operationalized through a suite of implementing guidelines, including the NDRRMC-DBM-DILG
Joint Memorandum Circular No. 2013-1 on LDRRMF allocation [10], DILG MC No. 2014-135 on LCCAP formulation
[11], and the NDRRMOC Standard Operating Procedures and Guidelines (SOPG) 2024 Edition [12]. Together, they
establish standardized templates, reporting protocols, and fiscal management rules applicable to all LGU tiers—
from provinces and cities to municipalities and barangays.
This report examines six interconnected frameworks that form the backbone of Philippine LGU climate and dis-
aster governance: the Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment (CDRA) methodology, the Local Climate Change
Action Plan (LCCAP) template, the LDRRMC/BDRRMC institutional structure, the LDRRMF allocation and Quick Re-
sponse Fund management system, the Damage Assessment and Needs Analysis (DANA) reporting protocol, and
theDRRMbudgetandauditframework—includingthefiscalimplicationsoftheMandanasrulingonLGUdisaster
funding.

6 DANA Forms and Reporting Protocol
The Damage Assessment and Needs Analysis (DANA) system is the Philippines’standardized post-disaster in-
formation framework, governed by the NDRRMOC Standard Operating Procedures and Guidelines (SOPG) 2024
## Edition [
12]. It operates through a tiered reporting approach with mandatory timelines and designated official
data sources for each category.
6.1 DANA Form 1: Initial Damage Assessment
The initial assessment (Flash/Initial Report) captures the immediate situation overview including disaster type,
date/time,affectedarea,andapproximatecasualtyfigures[26]. TheNDRRMCInitialReportcontainsstandardized
sections: Overview, Related Incidents (flooded areas, destructive winds by province), Affected Population (fami-
lies/persons inside/outside evacuation centers), Casualties (dead, injured, missing—validated vs. for-validation),
Roads and Bridges, Damaged Houses (with PHP cost), and Assistance Provided [
## 27].
6.2 DANA Form 2: Detailed Needs Analysis
The detailed assessment expands upon the initial report with sector-specific needs across search and rescue,
evacuation, protection, medical/health, shelter, food, water, sanitation, and lifeline systems—each with columns
for location, total affected, response status, unmet needs, and priority ranking [

The NDRRMOC SOPG 2024 mandates that the Initial Report shall be submittedwithin 3 hoursof receiving in-
formation, even if complete documentation is not yet available [12]. Reports are required when incidents meet
specificthresholds: atleast10casualtiesatregionallevelor50atnationallevel;atleast50familiesor250persons
affected/displaced; at least 50 houses damaged; or disruption to critical infrastructure and lifelines [12]. Reports
progress through three stages—Initial Report (within 3 hours), Progress Report (updates with substantiated doc-
umentation), and Terminal Report (final validated figures) [12].

Rapid DANA (RDANA) teams are multi-agency, typically comprising the DRRMO (overall coordinator), DSWD/

monitors and evaluates all disbursements, and upon LDRRMO recommendation and sanggunian approval, may
transfer funds to support other LDRRMCs declared under a state of calamity [

8 DRRM Budget Formats, COA Audit Guidelines, and Mandanas Fiscal Impacts
## 8.1 Standard Budget Reporting Format
TheFDP Form 8is the standard COA-prescribed template for LDRRMF utilization reporting, required for quar-
terly Full Disclosure Policy compliance by all LGUs [34]. The report structure includes three main components:
(A)SourcesofFunds—coveringcurrentappropriations, supplementalappropriations, continuingappropriations,
previous years’appropriations transferred to the Special Trust Fund, and transfers/grants from the NDRRMF or
other LGUs; (B) Utilization—broken down by the 70% mitigation and 30% QRF components with specific expendi-
ture line items such as medicines, food supplies, evacuation center repairs, trainings, equipment, and inter-LGU
transfers; and (C) Unutilized Balance [35,36].
8.2 COA Audit Framework
Two key COA circulars govern DRRM fund auditing.COA Circular No. 2014-002prescribes accounting and re-
porting guidelines for NDRRMF receipts and utilization, including journal entries, documentary requirements,
and financial reporting formats [33].COA Circular No. 2014-009establishes the audit framework for evaluating
DRRM fund compliance with RA 10121, covering allowable and disallowable expenditures [33]. COA has noted
that some LGUs“cut back on spending the local disaster risk reduction and management funds for they are too
afraid that such expenses”may be disallowed [
4]. COA has recommended that implementing offices develop
comprehensive programs of work for efficient disaster fund utilization, emphasizing that improved coordination
among agencies and enhanced project implementation strategies will enable disaster funds to better serve their
intended purpose [37].
## 8.3 Mandanas Ruling Fiscal Impact

the LGU share base to include all national tax collections—not just BIR-collected internal revenue taxes but also
customs duties and other collections—bringing LGUs to their full mandated 40% share [
1]. As shown in Figure 1,
the NTA jumped from PHP 695.49 billion in 2021 to PHP 959.04 billion in 2022 (a 23.9% increase in NTA, though

## 8.4 Utilization Challenges
Despite increased fiscal space, persistent underutilization remains a critical concern. LDRRMF utilization rates
ranged from 41% in FY 2018 to 66% in FY 2020 (driven by COVID-19 response), settling at 52% in FY 2021 [3].
Actual expenditures grew from PHP 9.51 billion in FY 2018 to PHP 28.44 billion in FY 2020 before declining to PHP
19.55 billion in FY 2021 [
